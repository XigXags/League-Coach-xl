# Fundamentals for every lane

## Summary

Every lane is one part of a single plan. Waves decide when a laner can leave, the jungle decides when a lane gets a second player, vision decides whether a move is safe, and objective timers decide where five players must be. A good call names who moves, when, and what happens if the expected wave or enemy is not there.

## Game phases

- Lane phase (roughly the first 14 minutes): laners trade for position, farm, and prepare first roams. Junglers clear and gank. Early neutral objectives and first tower falls decide the shape of the map.
- Mid game (roughly 14 to 25 minutes): side-lane pressure, tower trades, roams, and the next dragon or Herald windows. Wave timing and vision matter more than single duels.
- Late game (after about 25 minutes): fights decide games. Five-player positioning, objective timers, and the setup for Baron or Elder carry the match.

Exact timers and item values change by patch. Check MACRO_RESEARCH.md before quoting a number.

## Waves

- Fast push: the wave dies quickly under your tower. Use it to reset, recall, or punish an enemy who is out of lane.
- Slow push: the wave arrives slowly and piles up. Good for setting a timing, but the enemy can make it a trap if they have a bigger wave.
- Freeze: the wave stays near the middle of the lane or near your tower and the enemy must walk into it. Denies their XP and CS and forces them to come forward. Holding a freeze near your own tower is safe; holding it far forward is a gamble.
- Crash: the wave is fully pushed into the enemy tower so the enemy must respond. Use it to set up a roam, recall, or objective. A crash with no plan for the next 20 seconds is a wasted window.
- Wave-for-tower: once a wave reaches a tower and is cleared, the lane is open for a free tower hit. This is the most common conversion in the mid game.

A wave you cannot see is not a wave you know. Lane CS and level differences are clues about strength, not wave positions.

## Trades and all-ins

- A trade happens when both champions spend resources and one loses more health or position. Trade when the enemy key spell is down, when they stand where a return hit is possible, or when they miss the wave.
- An all-in needs a kill or a dive. Without both, it is a damage exchange that usually favours the player with more sustain or the better wave.
- Summoner spells: Flash, Teleport, Ignite, Smite, Heal, Barrier, Exhaust. The live feed lists the spells a player has equipped. It does not show whether they are ready. Track them by sight and by what the player used.

## Vision and information

- Vision denies the enemy the map. Wards take time to place and are lost quickly, so placement matters more than quantity.
- A laner missing from lane for too long usually means a roam or a recall. That is information about intent, not a known location.
- What the live feed gives: deaths and respawn timers, levels, CS, items, the active player's gold and health, objective events, and game time. It does not give wave positions, ward coverage, jungle camp state, or spell cooldowns. Say so when a call depends on them.

## Objectives

- Neutral objectives (dragon, Herald, Baron, Grubs when present) reward a team that arrives together. Set up with vision, lane pressure elsewhere, and the buffed player's safety in mind.
- Do not start an empty pit. Check the timer and the expected spawn first.
- Towers open lanes. A tower taken early changes the wave and the vision in the lane next to it.
- Baron and Elder: group before the fight, keep the buff holder alive, and leave when the buff ends or the numbers change.

## Positioning and movement

- Position is the trade. Two players at the same spot can have very different outcomes depending on who stands in front, who has a retreat path, and who can be reached first.
- Reach beats distance. An enemy's dash, blink, leap, or charge can end a position well beyond its auto-attack range, and a listed Flash or Teleport can do the same once it is ready. Count the reach a player has, not just the distance on the map.
- Movement tags in the feed (`movement_kit`) come from the champion's spell text. They are possibilities. Dashes are often conditional on a target, a cooldown, or a stack, so say "can reach" rather than "will reach".
- Stand behind the wave or the frontline, not in front of it. Stand where your own engage can reach the target and the enemy's reach cannot reach you first.
- Tower range is a hard edge. A player who walks into tower range with an enemy engage nearby is trading the tower's damage for the enemy's reach.
- River and fog entrances are where dives happen. A group standing at an entrance is visible to the other team only if they have vision. Say which entrance is covered and by whom.
- A player who is alone is in the wrong place unless the play says why. Name the ally who is in range before calling any solo position safe.
- The live feed does not show positions. Every positional statement must be framed as a plan or a possibility, and the call must name what would confirm it (a ward, a visible enemy, a respawn timer).

## Death and respawn

- Respawn timers grow with game time. A death gives the other team a numbers window. Count living players before choosing: five against four is a different game.
- After your own death, give up a small resource (a CS or a camp) to protect tempo. Do not chase into fog.

## Recalls and gold

- Recall after a wave is pushed, not in the middle of a fight. Spend gold as soon as a large amount is available; unspent gold is lost tempo.
- A recall that leaves the wave in the wrong place costs more than the item it buys.

## Mistakes that show up in every lane

- Forcing a fight right after a death.
- Starting an objective without vision or without the team.
- Crashing a wave with no roam or objective plan.
- Chasing one champion into fog.
- Treating CS or level leads as proof of wave control or map position.

## What a useful call names

- The observed trigger: who died, what time it is, which objective timer is near.
- Who moves, and along which path.
- The action and a deadline for leaving.
- The fallback if the expected wave, jungler, or enemy is not where the plan assumes.
