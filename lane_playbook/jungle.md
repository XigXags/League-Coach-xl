# Jungle

## Summary

The jungler's job is tempo and information. They choose which camps to clear, when to gank a lane, when to start an objective, and when to walk away. A gank works when a wave is near the lane's tower, the laner has their spells, and the jungler has come from fog without being seen. A dead or missing enemy jungler changes the map: check whether their Smite or ganks are still possible before committing. Never start an objective the team cannot cover.

## Job

- Clear the jungle efficiently: each camp should give the most value for the time spent.
- Choose lanes to gank by wave state, lane numbers, and whether the laner can use their spells.
- Start and set up neutral objectives with vision and a clear fallback.
- Track the enemy jungler by where they were, what they cleared, and which lane they entered.

## Mechanics

- Pathing: a route ends where it creates the most value. A path that clears a camp then arrives at a lane with the wave in your favour is a good path. A path that ends in fog with no wave is a bad one.
- Gank timing: arrive when the enemy's key spells are down or when the wave forces them to stand in a bad position. A gank from fog with the laner's spells ready is the strongest kind.
- Counter-gank: when the enemy jungler is in your lane, reading their path lets you walk to the other side and punish them. Missing their route costs you a lane.
- Smite: a listed Smite is a spell the player owns. It is only a fight tool when it is ready, and the feed does not tell you whether it is.
- Camps: camps respawn on timers. Check the current respawn timers before planning a path. The bot's objective timers come from the live event history, not from a camp feed.

## Early (lane phase)

- Choose a first clear that matches your champion and the enemy's lane. Start with the side where you are needed first.
- Gank the lane with the wave near its tower and the laner's spells up. Do not gank a lane where the wave is pushed away from you.
- Track the enemy jungler's first clear and their first gank direction. If they start on your side, expect a gank on your next move.
- Do not walk into fog alone when the laner is low on health or the wave is about to crash on them.

## Mid game

- Objective setup: start the dragon or Herald only when the adjacent lanes have pushed and vision is placed. An empty pit is a waste.
- Track which objectives are taken and by whom. The jungler knows the enemy's buff state better than the other roles.
- Counter-jungle: when the enemy jungler goes to the top side, the bot or mid can take the river or an objective at the other side.
- Do not clear a far camp while the team needs you in a lane fight.

## Late game

- Set up Baron or Elder with the team. The jungler is usually the one who starts the objective; the team must already be in position.
- When the enemy is grouped, the jungler's job is vision and peel for the carries, not chasing.
- Dead jungler: stop starting objectives. Use the respawn time to clear or to set up vision.

## Matchups

- Against a strong early jungle: clear the side they are not on first and gank the lane with the best wave.
- Against a late-game jungle: keep tempo and force them to choose between lanes and objectives.
- Against a jungler who ganks aggressively: play around the gank with vision and wave position rather than trading jungle for jungle.

## Reading the feed

- Deaths and respawn timers are real. A dead enemy jungler means no Smite and no gank from them for that timer.
- Level, CS, and items tell you about the enemy jungler's progress. They do not tell you where they are.
- Objective events show who took what. They do not show the camp state or the jungler's position.

## Common mistakes

- Ganking a lane with no wave near the tower.
- Starting an objective with the team split or without vision.
- Clearing a far camp while a lane is losing a fight.
- Walking into fog with no route plan.
- Losing track of the enemy jungler's last known side.

## What a useful call names

- The lane or objective, the path, and the spell or vision state the jungler needs.
- The arrival time or the timer that makes it safe.
- The exit: when to leave, and where the lane should be pushed before you go.
- The fallback if the expected enemy or wave is not where the plan assumes.
